# Practice and spaced repetition

Matiz is for people who already speak a language and want a richer vocabulary. Each edition starts with 68 practice words across four bands, plus 120 daily debuts. These 188 pilot questions record their origin and review status.

## Reviews

A round contains up to five words. Due reviews take priority over new words.

| Answer | Next scheduled review |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong or “I don't know” | After five other answers or ten minutes, whichever comes first |
| Correct on a scheduled retry | The next day |
| Later successful scheduled reviews | After 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, then every 30 days |

The answer count and time carry over between rounds and visits. An extra retry from free practice preserves the original review date. A word remains eligible for review after it is marked learned.

For new play, “Learned” records a successful scheduled review after an earlier correct answer. Old saves keep their previously fixed words and unfinished rounds. Matiz estimates the first review date for each previously seen word from its saved timestamp and progress.

## Daily words

Each daily set introduces four words, from easiest to hardest. The first 30 sets run from 17 November through 16 December 2026. At midnight in Lisbon, each set joins regular practice, even for players who missed it.

On unscheduled days, earlier sets loop in order as **Encore** or **Revisita**. A newly scheduled set takes priority. Web and Android use the same bundled calendar offline. Today's attempt and score are saved separately from practice.

## Evidence

Repeated retrieval improved delayed vocabulary recall in an experiment with word pairs. A separate spacing experiment found that the best review gap depended on how long the material had to be remembered. Neither study tested Matiz's multiple-choice game, level sizes or exact intervals. Those are pilot settings.

[Karpicke and Roediger](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408) · [Cepeda et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x)
